Conditioning

The matrix is only invertible, and well conditioned, if the true signals are diverse. If every measurement is taken with the same (for instance the sensor always lying flat), the columns multiplying , and are constant multiples of the bias columns and the bias becomes indistinguishable from the scale factors. Good data therefore needs the sensor held static in many different attitudes, covering all three axes in both directions. Measurement noise is averaged out only if the number of measurements exceeds the minimum, so use more than the bare minimum whenever possible.

Initial guess and convergence

LM only finds a local minimum, so the starting point matters. The calibrators take it from the caller (setInitialBias, setInitialMa, …​ or the constructor arguments); if nothing is given the starting point is and (an identity gain), which is a sensible default because real sensors are close to ideal. Robust estimation

Every non-trivial calibrator has five Robust…​ variants (see IMU Calibration for the family tree). They all follow the same pattern. A hypothesis is computed by running the plain calibrator on a small random subset of the measurements; the hypothesis is scored by how well it explains every measurement; the best hypothesis wins; optionally the winner is refined using all the measurements it classifies as inliers.

RANSAC

Random subsets. A measurement is an inlier when its residual is below threshold. The hypothesis with the most inliers wins. The number of iterations adapts to the requested confidence and the inlier ratio seen so far.

MSAC

Same random subsets, but the score is a truncated cost: every residual is clipped at threshold and the clipped residuals are summed (smaller is better). Inliers contribute their actual error instead of a flat vote, so among hypotheses with the same inlier count the tighter one wins.

Same as RANSAC

LMedS

Random subsets, scored by the median of all residuals (smaller is better). No inlier threshold is needed because the median is robust up to about half of outliers. The best median also yields the threshold used to classify inliers afterwards.

stopThreshold (1e-4): iteration stops early when the best median falls below it; confidence, maxIterations

PROSAC

Like RANSAC, but measurements are ordered by a quality score (larger is better) and subsets are drawn first from the best-ranked measurements, growing the pool progressively. Good measurements are found much sooner when scores are informative.

threshold, plus one quality score per measurement (QualityScoredAccelerometerCalibrator and the equivalents)

PROMedS

LMedS scoring with the PROSAC progressive sampling.

stopThreshold plus quality scores

The quality score of a measurement is any number you can attach to it (for example the inverse of its standard deviation, or the length of the static interval it came from); only the ordering matters.

Practical notes

  • The subset size defaults to the minimum number of measurements the plain calibrator needs and can be raised (setPreliminarySubsetSize), which makes each hypothesis less noisy at the cost of a lower chance of a clean sample.

  • The refinement step (refineResult, true by default) runs the plain calibrator on the inliers only, starting from the best hypothesis, and falls back to the unrefined hypothesis if it fails. The covariance is kept by default (keepCovariance).

  • The inlier flags and per-measurement residuals can be retained (computeAndKeepInliers, computeAndKeepResiduals) and are always available when refinement is on.

  • RANSAC and MSAC are sensitive to the threshold; LMedS and PROMedS need none, which is convenient when the noise level is not known beforehand.

The Tedaldi et al. method

Tedaldi, Pretto and Menegatti ([imu-tk]) describe a procedure that needs no turntable or external reference: the operator holds the IMU still for an initial period, then moves it between a set of distinct static attitudes. The accelerometer triad is calibrated from the static attitudes alone, and the gyroscope triad is then calibrated by checking that integrating the angular rate between two static attitudes reproduces the change of the gravity direction measured by the already calibrated accelerometer. The library splits this into detection, measurement generation and calibration stages.

Static-interval detection

Detection works on the accelerometer stream. After an initial period during which the device must not move (initialStaticSamples, default 5000 samples), the detector measures the base noise level: the norm of the per-axis variances accumulated over that period. For every new sample it computes the same variance norm over a sliding window (windowSize, default 101 samples) and applies a simple test:

where is the base noise level and is thresholdFactor (default 4.0). This is the detector of the paper/imu_tk, which compares the magnitude of the windowed variances with a multiple of the one measured in the initial period. Earlier versions of the library used the norm of the standard deviations (default factor 2.0); because the statistic is now based on variances, a factor on standard deviations corresponds to \(k_{\text{reference}} \approx k^{2}\) here, and every previously tuned factor changes meaning. As in imu_tk the window is centred: the status is assigned to the sample at the centre of the window, so every status change is delayed half a window (getDetectionDelaySamples()), in exchange for symmetric boundaries and static means free of motion samples. The paper estimates the multiplier automatically inside its calibration loop; the library instead exposes it as a parameter, and the generator documentation recommends repeating the calibration with several threshold factors, because the gyroscope calibration is very sensitive to where intervals start and end. Two safeguards act during initialization: if the windowed variance norm suddenly exceeds the accumulated one by instantaneousNoiseLevelFactor (default 9.0, it was 2.0), or the base level is above baseNoiseLevelAbsoluteThreshold (now a variance), the detector fails and must be reset, because the initial period was not still.

Measurements for the accelerometer

While the detector reports a static interval, the accumulated average of the accelerometer samples is the single input measurement of that interval:

When the interval ends (a transition to dynamic), AccelerometerMeasurementsGenerator emits one StandardDeviationBodyKinematics whose specific force is that mean and whose standard deviation is the average of the three per-axis deviations divided by (the standard deviation of a mean of samples). The initialization period is static, so its samples belong to the first static interval. Intervals shorter than minStaticSamples (default two windows) are discarded as unreliable, and a dynamic interval longer than maxDynamicSamples (default 30 windows) is skipped. Averaging suppresses the noise, so the noise term can be dropped from the model, as the paper does.

Gyroscope cost (integrated rotation vs. gravity direction)

The gyroscope calibration uses the dynamic intervals between consecutive static intervals. Let be the unit gravity direction measured by the calibrated accelerometer averaged in the -th static interval, and let be an attitude integrator that, given the angular-rate samples of the motion between intervals and and the starting direction , returns the predicted direction :

The integration advances a quaternion with the kinematics equation using a fourth-order Runge-Kutta step (the paper’s RK4n, followed by a re-normalization of the quaternion).

EasyGyroscopeCalibrator implements this cost as follows. Each sequence (one BodyKinematicsSequence: the mean specific force of the preceding static interval, the samples of the movement, and the mean specific force of the following static interval) is one observation:

  1. The two static means are corrected with the accelerometer bias and cross-coupling errors supplied by the user (that is why the calibrator is an AccelerometerDependentGyroscopeCalibrator) and normalized to unit vectors, so the result does not depend on the local gravity magnitude. The pair of directions is stored as the input row, and the target is zero.

  2. For the current parameter guess, every sample of the sequence is corrected with the gyroscope model, (the same reparametrization as before, with the g-dependent term when it is being estimated).

  3. The corrected angular rates are integrated with QuaternionIntegrator using the Runge-Kutta step to obtain the rotation over the sequence.

  4. The starting gravity direction is rotated by the inverse of that attitude change (the body frame turns while gravity is fixed) and compared with the measured end direction; the residual is the Euclidean distance between the two unit vectors.

The residual is the three-component difference (rather than the norm of that difference), weighted by the gravity-direction standard deviation of the sequence (BodyKinematicsSequence.getGravityDirectionStandardDeviation()), and minimized with LevenbergMarquardtMultiVariateFitter over , (and optionally ), using a numerical Jacobian from a JacobianEstimator with a relative and a minimum step of 1e-7. Differences from the paper that were kept: the library takes the gyroscope bias as an unknown of the fit (the paper averages the gyroscope in the initial still period and removes it), and it has an optional upper-triangular common z-axis form. Following the paper, the defaults of EasyGyroscopeCalibrator are now the general (full) matrix and no g-dependent term (commonAxisUsed = false, estimateGDependentCrossBiases = false); the upper-triangular form is only valid for the sensor defining the body frame (the accelerometer), and the g-dependent matrix is weakly observable from gravity references only. Putting it together

  1. Keep the device still for the initial period (long enough for initialStaticSamples).

  2. Move it to a new attitude and hold it still, many times, producing alternating static and dynamic intervals.

  3. Feed every sample through an AccelerometerAndGyroscopeMeasurementsGenerator (or the individual accelerometer and gyroscope generators); collect the generated StandardDeviationBodyKinematics and BodyKinematicsSequence instances.

  4. Calibrate the accelerometer with KnownGravityNorm/KnownPosition using the first collection.

  5. Pass the estimated accelerometer bias and Ma plus the sequences to EasyGyroscopeCalibrator (or KnownBiasEasyGyroscopeCalibrator when the gyroscope bias is known), optionally wrapped by a robust variant.

See Static/Dynamic Interval Detection and Measurement Generation for the detector and generator classes in detail.
