Static/Dynamic Interval Detection and Measurement Generation

This documentation was generated with the assistance of AI. Please report any inaccuracies.

Classes: com.irurueta.navigation.inertial.calibration.intervals (detection, 9 classes), com.irurueta.navigation.inertial.calibration.generators (measurement building, 13 classes), plus the root-level BodyKinematicsGenerator, BodyMagneticFluxDensityGenerator, TimeIntervalEstimator, and TimeIntervalEstimatorListener (see the Class Reference table at the end of this page for Javadoc and source links).

This is the preprocessing step behind the calibrators in Accelerometer Calibration and Gyroscope Calibration that don’t need a turntable or precisely known frames — most directly, [imu-tk]'s "Easy" gyroscope calibrator and the accelerometer’s gravity-norm family. Both need the raw sensor stream split into static periods (device held still, mean specific force reveals the local gravity direction) and dynamic periods (device being moved between orientations) — without any external ground truth telling them which is which.

TriadStaticIntervalDetector: classifying static vs. dynamic

The core class, TriadStaticIntervalDetector (specialized as AccelerationTriadStaticIntervalDetector, AngularSpeedTriadStaticIntervalDetector, MagneticFluxDensityTriadStaticIntervalDetector), consumes one triad per call to process(…​). The generators of this page always drive an AccelerationTriadStaticIntervalDetector: the accelerometer stream decides which samples are static, for every sensor. The detector implements the static-interval test described in the Tedaldi et al. section, with the differences listed at the end of this section.

Statistics used

Two noise estimators from the noise package run side by side:

  • A windowed estimator keeps the last samples (windowSize, default 101; it must be odd and at least 3). For every new sample it recomputes, over the window only, the per-axis mean and the unbiased per-axis variance (division by , where is the number of samples actually stored in the window, which is once the window is full; before that the statistics are computed over the stored samples and a single sample gives a variance of 0). The detector uses the variance norm of the window, the same statistic as imu_tk:

    The window only counts as valid once it holds samples, so no decision is taken before that.

  • An accumulated estimator keeps a running mean and standard deviation of every sample added to it since its last reset. During initialization it is fed every sample; afterwards it is fed only the samples classified as static and is reset each time a static interval ends.

The previous standard-deviation based quantity remains available as the base noise standard deviation norm. After a failure the status is FAILED, process(…​) returns false and ignores further samples, and the only way out is reset(), which also clears the counters, base noise level and both estimators.

Steady-state classification

From then on, samples are classified by comparing the windowed variance norm with the threshold. The window is centred: the sample that receives the status is the one at the centre of the window, not the latest one,

so every status change is reported getDetectionDelaySamples() samples (half a window) after the sample where it happened. In exchange, boundaries are symmetric: a dynamic interval starts about half a window before the statistic of a trailing window would exceed the threshold, and a static interval starts about half a window after the motion ends, so the static means no longer contain motion samples. Listener callbacks and the accumulated averages refer to the classified (delayed) sample, whereas the getInstantaneous* values still describe the current window. The initialization behaviour (INITIALIZING, INITIALIZATION_COMPLETED) is unchanged.

While the status is static, the sample is also added to the accumulated estimator, whose mean and standard deviation will describe that interval once it ends. Listener callbacks fire only when the status changes (the transition rules are in the diagram below):

  • onStaticIntervalDetected fires when a static interval starts (including the first one after initialization), and receives the current window mean and standard deviation.

  • onDynamicIntervalDetected fires when a dynamic interval starts, that is when the static interval before it has just ended. It receives the window values and also the accumulated mean and standard deviation of the static interval that just finished, and then the accumulated estimator is reset.

Because the decision uses a full window of samples that is centred on the classified sample, boundaries are reported with a fixed delay of about samples (getDetectionDelaySamples()). Generators use it to align their items with the classified samples. windowSize should still cover about one to two seconds of data, and the generators below discard intervals shorter than a couple of windows.

Differences from the paper

  • Variance norm, as in imu_tk. The detector compares the norm of the windowed per-axis variances with times the same norm of the base period, as the paper/imu_tk does. Earlier versions used the norm of the standard deviations, and a factor on standard deviations corresponds to a factor \(k_{\text{reference}} \approx k^{2}\) on the variance norm. Every thresholdFactor, instantaneousNoiseLevelFactor and absolute threshold you tuned for a previous version changes meaning: square the former standard-deviation factors (the default thresholdFactor went from 2.0 to 4.0). The power spectral densities reported by the detectors are still derived from the standard deviation based base noise level.

  • Centred window. As in imu_tk the classified sample is the one at the centre of the window (see above), which delays every status change by half a window.

  • Fixed factor. The paper tunes the multiplier automatically inside its calibration loop. Here it is the thresholdFactor parameter, and the generator Javadoc recommends repeating the gyroscope calibration with several values, because the gyroscope result depends on where intervals start and end.

  • Extra safeguards. The two failure checks above are additions of the library.

MeasurementsGenerator: building calibration inputs from intervals

MeasurementsGenerator wraps an AccelerationTriadStaticIntervalDetector and reacts to its transitions to build the objects the calibrators consume. Its constructor-time defaults are:

Parameter Default Meaning

minStaticSamples

202 (2 windows of 101)

A static interval with fewer samples is skipped (no measurement) and does not provide a gravity reference. Setter minimum is 3.

maxDynamicSamples

3030 (30 windows of 101)

A dynamic interval longer than this is skipped. Setter minimum is 3.

windowSize, initialStaticSamples, thresholdFactor, …​

as in the detector

Forwarded to the internal detector (getWindowSize(), getInitialStaticSamples() and so on).

The two defaults are computed from the default window size; if you change windowSize they do not follow it, so set them explicitly. The skip rules work on sample counters:

  • While the status is static, the static counter grows and the dynamic counter is set to 0, and vice versa.

  • When a dynamic interval starts, the static interval that just ended is skipped if the static counter is below minStaticSamples, and onStaticIntervalSkipped is raised.

  • The initialization period is static, so its samples are part of the first static interval: the average of that interval includes the initialization samples (the accumulated estimator is not reset when initialization completes), and they also count towards minStaticSamples, for every generator.

  • If the dynamic counter exceeds maxDynamicSamples, the current dynamic interval is flagged as skipped (once, with onDynamicIntervalSkipped) until the next static interval begins. A gyroscope sequence made of joined dynamic intervals is discarded in the same way when its total number of items exceeds maxDynamicSamples.

  • Noise levels and thresholds are variance-based as plain double values; their measurement-typed variants (…​AsMeasurement) are the square root of those values, expressed in the unit of the sensor.

Averaging: what a measurement contains

At the end of a kept static interval of samples, with the accumulated per-axis estimates and of that interval:

AccelerometerMeasurementsGenerator emits a StandardDeviationBodyKinematics with specific force , zero angular rate and specific-force standard deviation : the plain average of the three axes (not their norm) divided by , because the standard deviation of the mean of samples is (previously the undivided average was reported). For the first interval includes the initialization samples. The averaging is what allows the noise term to be dropped from the model in the Tedaldi et al. formulation; the standard deviation is kept so that calibrators can weight or filter measurements.

Sequences for the gyroscope

GyroscopeMeasurementsGenerator needs the motion between two static intervals. It records every sample of a dynamic interval as a StandardDeviationTimedBodyKinematics (kinematics, timestamp, plus two standard deviations taken from the initial static period). Sequences follow the rules of imu_tk:

  • A sequence needs a valid static interval (at least minStaticSamples samples) on both sides. If a static interval is too short, it provides no gravity reference and the dynamic intervals on both of its sides are joined into a single sequence. A sequence whose preceding static interval has no samples is discarded.

  • A sequence is emitted when the static interval that follows it ends, so that its afterMeanSpecificForce is the accumulated average of that entire static interval (previously it was the last window at the moment the static interval was detected). The same average is the beforeMeanSpecificForce of the next sequence. Consequently, when a recording ends while the device is at rest, the last sequence is still pending: call flush() once no more samples are going to be processed to generate it from the static samples classified so far (at least minStaticSamples).

  • The initialization period is static: its average (initialization samples included) is the first beforeMeanSpecificForce, so a motion starting right after initialization never gets a (0, 0, 0) reference.

  • The items of the sequence are aligned with the samples classified as dynamic by the centred-window detector, taking getDetectionDelaySamples() into account.

  • Each sequence carries a gravity direction standard deviation (BodyKinematicsSequence.getGravityDirectionStandardDeviation() / setGravityDirectionStandardDeviation(…​), dimensionless, default 0, included in copyFrom, equals and hashCode):

where is the accelerometer noise standard deviation, the number of averaged samples of the static intervals before and after, the gyroscope noise root PSD (rad/s/sqrt(Hz)) and the duration of the sequence, so that the integrated gyroscope noise term is dimensionless. EasyGyroscopeCalibrator uses it as the standard deviation of its three-component residual, see Gyroscope Calibration.

The two mean specific forces are the start and end gravity directions of the -th motion, the quantities the integration cost of the Tedaldi et al. method compares with the integrated gyroscope rotation. A dynamic interval flagged as skipped produces no sequence. The gyroscope generator applies minStaticSamples only to decide which static intervals are valid gravity references (as described above). The accelerometer and magnetometer generators drop short static intervals.

The magnetometer generator averages the field during static samples (and during initialization), and clears its estimator whenever the status is dynamic, so its mean covers the same kind of interval as the accelerometer’s.

AccelerometerMeasurementsGenerator generator = new AccelerometerMeasurementsGenerator(
        (gen, measurement) -> measurements.add(measurement));

// feed raw BodyKinematics samples as they arrive from the IMU
for (BodyKinematics sample : rawKinematicsStream) {
    generator.process(sample);
}
// 'measurements' now holds one StandardDeviationBodyKinematics per detected static
// interval, ready for calibration/accelerometer.adoc's gravity-norm calibrators

TimeIntervalEstimator

A small, separate utility: it estimates the average sampling period, and its variance, of a stream of timestamps in seconds, so that the timeInterval of the noise estimators, the detector and the generators can be set from measured data instead of nominal values. The estimator is finite: it stops after totalSamples timestamps (default 100000, so about 33 minutes of a 50 Hz stream), isFinished() becomes true, and further timestamps are ignored (addTimestamp returns false). reset() clears it. Listener callbacks are onStart, onTimestampAdded, onFinish and onReset.

for (double timestampSeconds : rawTimestamps) {
    estimator.addTimestamp(timestampSeconds);
}

double timeInterval = estimator.getAverageTimeInterval(); // seconds, e.g. ~0.02 for a 50 Hz IMU
double timeIntervalStd = estimator.getTimeIntervalStandardDeviation();

Root-level generators: synthetic test data (a different purpose)

BodyKinematicsGenerator and BodyMagneticFluxDensityGenerator are unrelated to interval detection — they go the other direction, synthesizing noisy, uncalibrated measurements from known-true kinematics/field plus a known IMUErrors model, for testing calibrators and fixers against ground truth. They follow [book-groves] and its companion IMU_model.m script rather than the Tedaldi et al. method.
